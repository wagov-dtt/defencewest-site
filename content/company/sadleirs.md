## Overview

Sadleirs Global Logistics is part of Sadleirs, a privately owned Australian business with origins in Fremantle dating back to 1829. The Group brings together the heritage of Lionel Samson & Son and R.C. Sadleir, established in 1895 as a transport, shipping and customs clearing company. Today, Sadleirs Global supports defence, government, aerospace and sovereign industry customers through Australian-controlled logistics capability across international freight, customs, regulated cargo, critical movements and domestic supply chains. Headquartered in Western Australia and wholly Australian owned, Sadleirs retains strategic direction, operational control and commercial accountability in Australia. This provides jurisdictional clarity for customers operating in sensitive, regulated and high-assurance environments.
## Capabilities

Sadleirs Global Logistics provides defence-relevant capability across global freight forwarding, air and sea freight, air and sea charter solutions, customs brokerage, quarantine clearance, regulated air cargo, critical cargo, project cargo, secure warehousing, road and rail coordination, and heavy transport. Its role is often to determine the appropriate mode, route, clearance pathway, handling arrangement and compliance approach for complex or sensitive freight.
 
Recent examples of relevant capability include the coordination and handling of a complex, high-value helicopter movement through Fremantle Port for onward delivery in Western Australia, demonstrating experience in aviation cargo, port handling, specialist freight coordination and sensitive movement planning. 
 
Through the broader Sadleirs Group, the business can also draw on secure warehousing, laydown and handling capability, a national fleet, on-site workshop support, specialist road and rail services, and heavy transport assets including 50-tonne trailers. This supports high-value, oversized, regulated and defence-relevant freight movements across metropolitan, regional and remote operating environments.
